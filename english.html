WhisperNormalizer English Module

OpenAI’s English text standardisation module

What does this module do?

As per the text normalization/standardization approach Appendix Section C pp.21 the paper Robust Speech Recognition via Large-Scale Weak Supervision. The EnglishTextNormalizer does the following functionality:

  1. Remove any phrases between matching brackets ([, ]).
  2. Remove any phrases between matching parentheses ((, )).
  3. Remove any of the following words: hmm, mm, mhm, mmm, uh, um
  4. Remove whitespace characters that comes before an apostrophe ’
  5. Convert standard or informal contracted forms of English into the original form.
  6. Remove commas (,) between digits
  7. Remove periods (.) not followed by numbers
  8. Remove symbols as well as diacritics from the text, where symbols are the characters with the Unicode category starting with M, S, or P, except period, percent, and currency symbols that may be detected in the next step.
  9. Detect any numeric expressions of numbers and currencies and replace with a form using Arabic numbers, e.g. “Ten thousand dollars” → “$10000”.
  10. Convert British spellings into American spellings.
  11. Remove remaining symbols that are not part of any numeric expressions.
  12. Replace any successive whitespace characters with a space.

EnglishNumberNormalizer

def EnglishNumberNormalizer():

Convert any spelled-out numbers into arabic numbers, while handling:

  • remove any commas
  • keep the suffixes such as: 1960s, 274th, 32nd, etc.
  • spell out currency symbols after the number. e.g. $20 million -> 20000000 dollars
  • spell out one and ones
  • interpret successive single-digit numbers as nominal: one oh one -> 101

EnglishTextNormalizer

def EnglishTextNormalizer(
    tts_mode:bool=False
):

Applies all the rules for normalizing English text as mentioned in OpenAI whisper paper. As per the text normalization/standardization approach Appendix Section C pp.21 the paper Robust Speech Recognition via Large-Scale Weak Supervision. The EnglishTextNormalizer does the following functionality:

  1. Remove any phrases between matching brackets ([, ]).
  2. Remove any phrases between matching parentheses ((, )).
  3. Remove any of the following words: hmm, mm, mhm, mmm, uh, um
  4. Remove whitespace characters that comes before an apostrophe ’
  5. Convert standard or informal contracted forms of English into the original form; 's is “is” after personal pronouns and wh-words; after indefinite pronouns it is “is” only when the next word starts a predicate (someone's here), and possessive before a noun (someone's car).
  6. Remove commas (,) between digits
  7. Remove periods (.) not followed by numbers
  8. Remove symbols as well as diacritics from the text, where symbols are the characters with the Unicode category starting with M, S, or P, except period, percent, and currency symbols that may be detected in the next step.
  9. Detect any numeric expressions of numbers and currencies and replace with a form using Arabic numbers, e.g. “Ten thousand dollars” → “$10000”.
  10. Convert British spellings into American spellings.
  11. Remove remaining symbols that are not part of any numeric expressions.
  12. Replace any successive whitespace characters with a space.

With tts_mode=True (requires pip install whisper_normalizer[tts]) the text is prepared for speech instead: step 9 is skipped so written numbers stay words, digits, currencies, percentages and decimals are spelled out, and &, +, = and @ are spoken as words. A leading - is spoken as minus, phone numbers are read digit by digit, and decades such as 1990s are spoken as words.

Where this package intentionally differs

Two Whisper number-normalizer bugs are fixed here, and they are not covered by the upstream tests above: "one thousand million" keeps its million, and a failed currency conversion returns the match instead of the whole string. The tts_mode cases further down are additions upstream does not have.

# Upstream returns "1000" here: the multiplier is dropped.
assert EnglishNumberNormalizer()("one thousand million") == "1000000000"

# A "1" inside another token (money, percent, decimals) is not rewritten.
text = EnglishTextNormalizer()
assert text("I paid $1") == "i paid $1"
assert text("1%") == "1%"
assert text("3.1") == "3.1"
assert text("he is 1") == "he is one"

tts_mode for English

With tts_mode=True the text is prepared for speech: written numbers stay words (the usual words-to-digits step is skipped), digits, currencies and percentages are spelled out, and a few symbols are spoken. Default behaviour is unchanged.

# tts_mode spells digits as words.
tts = EnglishTextNormalizer(tts_mode=True)
assert tts("I have 3 apples and 42 pears") == "i have three apples and forty two pears"
# Written numbers stay words in tts_mode: the usual words-to-digits step is skipped.
assert tts("I was born in nineteen eighty four") == "i was born in nineteen eighty four"
assert tts("a hundred apples") == "a hundred apples"

# Default behaviour is unchanged: tts_mode is off, and words still become digits.
default = EnglishTextNormalizer()
assert default.tts_mode is False
assert default("I was born in nineteen eighty four") == "i was born in 1984"
assert default("a hundred apples") == "a 100 apples"
assert default("I have three apples and 42 pears") == "i have 3 apples and 42 pears"

# Apostrophe handling is shared with default mode.
assert tts("It’s John’s car") == "it is johns car"
assert tts("Everyone's here") == "everyone is here"
# tts_mode: a currency symbol is spoken as the amount followed by the unit (singular for one).
for text, expected in [
    ("It costs $20", "it costs twenty dollars"),
    ("just $1", "just one dollar"),
    ("$1,200 total", "one thousand two hundred dollars total"),
    ("€5.50 each", "five euros fifty cents each"),
    ("£2.01", "two pounds one penny"),
    ("£3", "three pounds"),
    ("only 5¢", "only five cents"),
]:
    assert tts(text) == expected, (text, tts(text), expected)
# tts_mode: percentages and decimals are spoken (decimals digit by digit), and a few symbols become words.
for text, expected in [
    ("50% off", "fifty percent off"),
    ("3.5% growth", "three point five percent growth"),
    ("pi is 3.14", "pi is three point one four"),
    ("exactly 3.50", "exactly three point five zero"),
    ("salt & pepper", "salt and pepper"),
    ("2 + 2 = 4", "two plus two equals four"),
    ("meet me @ noon", "meet me at noon"),
]:
    assert tts(text) == expected, (text, tts(text), expected)
# Without the optional num2words package, tts_mode fails loudly with install instructions; default mode is unaffected.
import sys

_saved = sys.modules.get("num2words")
sys.modules["num2words"] = None  # makes `import num2words` raise ImportError
try:
    try:
        EnglishTextNormalizer(tts_mode=True)
        raise AssertionError("expected ImportError")
    except ImportError as e:
        assert "whisper_normalizer[tts]" in str(e)
    assert EnglishTextNormalizer()("I have three apples") == "i have 3 apples"
finally:
    if _saved is None:
        sys.modules.pop("num2words", None)
    else:
        sys.modules["num2words"] = _saved
# tts_mode: a price under one unit is just its minor unit; ordinals are spoken as ordinals.
for text, expected in [
    ("It costs $0.05", "it costs five cents"),
    ("£0.01", "one penny"),
    ("$0", "zero dollars"),
    ("the 1st and 2nd place", "the first and second place"),
    ("on the 21st, 3rd and 11th", "on the twenty first third and eleventh"),
    ("the 100th day", "the one hundredth day"),
]:
    assert tts(text) == expected, (text, tts(text), expected)

# tts_mode: signs, phone numbers and decades are spoken as words, not digits.
for text, expected in [
    ("-5 degrees", "minus five degrees"),
    ("call 555-1234", "call five five five one two three four"),
    ("call 555-123-4567", "call five five five one two three four five six seven"),
    ("the 1990s", "the nineteen nineties"),
    ("the 2010s", "the twenty tens"),
    ("the 2000s", "the two thousands"),
]:
    assert tts(text) == expected, (text, tts(text), expected)
